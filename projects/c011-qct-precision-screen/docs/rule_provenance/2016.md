# 2016 rule provenance and counterfactual identification

## Public fields and transformations

- Three ACS releases: suffixes 11, 12 and 13.
- Income precision passes only when the positive B19013 estimate and MoE are present and
  `MoE < c × estimate` (strict inequality).
- The release income score is the public adjusted-income-limit intermediate divided by the
  raw B19013 estimate.
- Poverty precision passes only when both positive B17001 denominator/numerator estimates
  and MoEs are present and both satisfy the same strict screen.
- Positive poverty rates use round-half-up to three decimals, matching the retained workbook.
- Unreliable releases are excluded from annual averages.
- Income annual ranking averages are rounded to six decimals; poverty averages to three.
- Criterion ranks are average ranks over the entire statutory allocation area before the
  both-criteria bonus is added.
- Final ordering uses combined rank, then population; allocation is greedy
  skip-and-continue under the rounded 20% population cap.

## Equivalence-class adjudication

Three independently defensible treatments of the public income score were frozen before
the c=1.00 comparison: unrounded division, seven-decimal release rounding, and using retained
stored values where available with raw division only for newly admitted releases. Each has
zero c=.50 mismatches. All three yield byte-identical E/Q set digests and the same five-part
decomposition under c=1.00. The 2016 counterfactual is therefore **identified** from retained
public provenance despite low-level arithmetic nonuniqueness.

The regenerated BRD is `0.12239634180436126`, versus the earlier diagnostic
`0.1223915232729`; the difference is 0.0000048185 in probability units and does not change
any record-level E/Q set or substantive conclusion.
