## Chosen research neighborhood

> **Reliability and transportability of statistical/ML models under imperfect real-world data**

The initial emphasis is biomedical/health data, but the statistical problems are intended to generalize.

## Mapping pass 1 — selected spine

The first deep mapping pass deliberately focuses on:

> **External predictive reliability under measurement / observation-process shift**

This is nested inside dataset shift rather than treated as an independent field.

### Why this spine was selected

It connects all of the program’s core interests without prematurely committing to one model class:

- distribution shift;
- calibration failure;
- missingness and missingness shift;
- workflow/informative observation;
- external validation and transportability;
- subgroup reliability;
- monitoring and updating.

It also exposes an important interface between **classical clinical prediction methodology** and **modern robust/distribution-shift ML**.

## Central scientific question

> When should we trust a prediction model outside its development environment when not only the patient population, but also **what is measured, when it is measured, and how it enters the dataset** can change?

A generic source/target framing is:

\[
P_s(X,Y) \neq P_t(X,Y),
\]

but for routine clinical data this is incomplete. Let \(R\) denote the recording/measurement process: variable availability, test ordering, observation timing, measurement frequency, interface availability, latency, and related workflow features. Then external deployment may involve:

\[
P_s(X,Y,R) \neq P_t(X,Y,R).
\]

The central practical danger is that a model may learn predictive information carried by \(R\) in the source environment that is not stable in the target environment.

# 1. Field architecture: four interacting communities

The literature does not form one unified community. Mapping the interfaces is necessary to avoid false novelty.

## Community A — Clinical prediction-model methodology

### Core objects

- external validation;
- calibration-in-the-large/intercept;
- calibration slope and flexible curves;
- discrimination;
- Brier/log scores;
- clinical usefulness / net benefit;
- model updating;
- deployment-compatible handling of missing predictors.

### What this community contributes

This literature provides the strongest evaluation discipline. It explicitly separates calibration from discrimination and treats prediction-model validation as more than AUROC [R010–R020].

### Limitation relative to our focus

Measurement heterogeneity and missing-predictor handling are recognized, but the literature is not generally organized around a unified **observation-process shift** object that can be stress-tested across modern ML pipelines.

## Community B — Distribution-shift / robust ML

### Core objects

- covariate shift;
- prior/label shift;
- concept/conditional shift;
- subpopulation shift;
- robustness/stability under user-specified shifts;
- target-performance estimation;
- adaptation/reweighting.

### What this community contributes

Formal taxonomies, robustness estimands, shift-specific assumptions, stress-testing ideas, target-risk estimation and adaptation methods [R001–R007, R032–R038].

### Limitation relative to our focus

A generic \(P(X,Y)\) taxonomy can obscure that clinical inputs are generated by a healthcare system. “Covariate shift” can conflate physiology, demographics, coding, measurement policy and workflow.

## Community C — Informative presence / observation / missingness

### Core objects

- informative presence;
- informative observation;
- visit processes;
- test-ordering/measurement processes;
- missingness mechanisms;
- missingness shift;
- clinical presence shift;
- observation intensity as a predictive feature.

### What this community contributes

It establishes that EHR recording patterns are informative and can reflect patient state plus healthcare processes [R021–R028]. Recent work now explicitly studies robustness to missingness shifts and **clinical presence shift**.

### Limitation relative to our focus

Much of the literature addresses estimation, prediction methods or a single type of shift rather than a general external-validation diagnostic that attributes reliability loss to the observation process while evaluating calibration and clinical utility.

## Community D — Deployment monitoring / lifecycle reliability

### Core objects

- performance drift;
- calibration drift;
- label delay;
- data latency;
- input availability;
- operational telemetry;
- reactive/proactive updating.

### What this community contributes

It demonstrates that reliability is not static after deployment and that workflow telemetry may provide early warnings [R030, R039–R041].

### Limitation relative to our focus

Monitoring after deployment is not the same scientific problem as **pre-deployment external stress testing and attribution**.

# 2. Core result of the map

## The promising seam is narrower than “missingness shift”

The evidence does **not** support a claim that missingness, informative observation, calibration under shift, or external validation are neglected topics.

They are active literatures.

The potentially under-connected seam is:

> **How should external validation diagnose when predictive reliability depends on a source-specific measurement / observation process, rather than only on patient case mix or physiological covariates?**

A useful contribution would need to connect:

1. a scientifically explicit shift decomposition;
2. external-validation design;
3. calibration and decision utility as primary reliability outcomes;
4. real multi-site EHR provenance;
5. controlled stress tests or other design elements capable of separating observation-process dependence from generic source/target difference.

This is currently a **candidate-generating inference**, not a validated literature gap.

# 3. What is already known strongly enough to constrain candidate design

## 3.1 Calibration can fail without dramatic discrimination failure

Clinical prediction methodology treats calibration as a separate property from discrimination [R010–R012]. Temporal and external-validation literatures repeatedly show that probability reliability can deteriorate even when ranking remains usable [R009, R011, R027, R030–R031].

**Constraint:** a study based only on AUROC is scientifically inadequate for this program.

## 3.2 Measurement procedures can alter external performance

Predictor measurement heterogeneity changes model performance and can produce substantial calibration differences across settings [R016–R017].

**Constraint:** source/target differences in variable definitions, instruments, thresholds or measurement procedures cannot be treated as incidental preprocessing noise.

## 3.3 Missing-data handling must match deployment reality

Clinical prediction work shows that handling missing predictors during validation/application requires deployment-compatible strategies [R018–R020]. The 2024 Manchester thesis directly develops this pipeline logic [R020].

**Constraint:** a project cannot claim novelty merely because it studies missing values at external validation.

## 3.4 Observation patterns are themselves predictive

Test ordering, test timing, healthcare contact and missingness can encode healthcare-system processes [R021–R023].

**Constraint:** “missing” is not always missing-at-random nuisance information. Removing or exploiting missingness can both be wrong depending on the target use and transportability of the mechanism.

## 3.5 Missingness-shift robustness is an active methods frontier

Rockenschaub et al. distinguish ignorable and non-ignorable missingness shifts theoretically [R024]. MIRRAMS develops an algorithm specifically for unseen missingness shifts [R025]. Jeanselme et al. formalize **clinical presence shift** and propose a joint model [R026].

**Constraint:** a generic “new model robust to missingness shift” is low-priority for the first project.

## 3.6 The closest direct competitor already tests workflow-feature transportability

Yamamoto et al. 2026 evaluate sepsis mortality prediction across MIMIC-IV and eICU-CRD and report that richer observation-process features can improve internal discrimination while worsening external calibration/transportability [R027].

**Constraint:** a simple paper showing “measurement counts hurt transfer” would be redundant.

## 3.7 Generic cross-site calibration is also becoming crowded

Patel & Beedala 2026 perform MIMIC-IV→eICU external ICU mortality validation with calibration, recalibration, decision curves and subgroup analyses [R031].

**Constraint:** “evaluate AUROC + calibration + net benefit across MIMIC/eICU” is not enough differentiation.

## 3.8 Generic post-deployment monitoring is no longer an open field

Kopanitsa 2026 analyzes four deployed clinical AI systems and reports calibration drift plus operational telemetry signals including missingness and latency [R030].

**Constraint:** a generic label-free missingness/latency monitoring paper is not the strongest first project.

# 4. Candidate scientific decomposition

A source-to-target failure can arise from multiple processes that should not be collapsed into one label.

## 4.1 Case-mix shift

Changes in patient characteristics and disease severity:

\[
P_s(Z) \neq P_t(Z),
\]

where \(Z\) denotes clinically meaningful latent/observed patient state.

## 4.2 Outcome/base-rate shift

Changes in prevalence or outcome incidence:

\[
P_s(Y) \neq P_t(Y).
\]

This can strongly affect calibration even with otherwise useful discrimination.

## 4.3 Physiological/predictor-value shift

Changes in the distribution of measured values:

\[
P_s(X_{obs}) \neq P_t(X_{obs}).
\]

## 4.4 Predictor measurement-procedure shift

The same underlying construct is measured differently due to instrument, assay, threshold, unit, coding, timing or abstraction changes [R016–R017].

## 4.5 Observation-process shift

The probability/timing/intensity of observing a variable changes:

\[
P_s(R \mid Z, X, Y, H) \neq P_t(R \mid Z, X, Y, H),
\]

where \(H\) can include site/workflow history.

Examples:

- different laboratory-ordering policies;
- different vital-sign charting frequency;
- different nurse/clinician workflow;
- different interface availability;
- different data latency;
- different patient-contact process;
- different missingness mechanism.

## 4.6 Label-observation shift

Outcome labels themselves may be delayed, selectively observed, or defined differently [R033].

### Design implication

A useful reliability framework should state which of these processes it seeks to diagnose and which remain entangled. Calling all of them “dataset shift” is not sufficient.

# 5. Reliability outcomes that should anchor this program

For binary clinical prediction, the minimum evaluation set should usually include:

## Probability reliability

- calibration-in-the-large / intercept;
- calibration slope;
- flexible calibration curve;
- Brier score or other proper scoring rule;
- log loss where appropriate.

## Ranking/discrimination

- AUROC;
- AUPRC when prevalence makes it informative.

## Clinical utility

- decision-curve/net benefit over prespecified clinically plausible thresholds;
- threshold-specific sensitivity/specificity/PPV/NPV when tied to actual use.

## Heterogeneity

- site-level performance;
- clinically prespecified subgroup calibration/performance;
- site × subgroup interaction when powered.

## Degradation quantities

For metric \(M\):

\[
\Delta M = M_{target} - M_{source}.
\]

But candidate C008 should go further and ask whether some component of \(\Delta M\) is attributable to observation-process dependence rather than generic source/target difference.

# 6. Data map

## MIMIC-IV [R042]

**Role:** mature source environment, rich clinical variables, strong reproducibility ecosystem.  
**Strength:** broad use allows comparison with prior work.  
**Weakness:** one health system; limited natural site heterogeneity.

## eICU-CRD [R043]

**Role:** primary natural laboratory for multi-site/unit observation-process heterogeneity.  
**Strength:** >200k ICU admissions across many hospitals/units; documentation explicitly notes that data-type availability can depend on whether a unit has a corresponding interface.  
**Scientific importance:** the recorded dataset can therefore differ because of **data infrastructure/workflow**, not only because the patient was unmeasured physiologically.  
**Weakness:** heterogeneous data provenance and access restrictions require careful design.

## eICU demo [R044]

**Role:** SQL/schema prototyping before full credentialed extraction.  
**Not suitable:** final inference.

## HiRID [R045]

**Role:** possible independent European high-resolution ICU environment.  
**Value:** tests whether findings survive beyond the dominant MIMIC/eICU pair.

## AmsterdamUMCdb [R046]

**Role:** possible European external environment with OMOP representation.

## BlendedICU [R047–R048]

**Role:** harmonized multi-database substrate explicitly designed for generalizability studies.  
**Caution:** this project’s target is partly **how data were generated**. Harmonization is useful for common variable definitions, but source provenance and original measurement/availability structures must be retained. The claim that harmonization can mask the process of interest is a project inference, not a result established by R047.

# 10. Priority candidate: C008

## Working title

**Measurement-process-aware external-validation stress testing for clinical prediction models**

## Working question

> When does predictive information encoded by missingness, measurement intensity, or other observation-process features improve internal model performance but make predicted risk non-transportable, and how should external validation diagnose that failure?

## Why it matters

A prediction model can learn two kinds of signal:

1. relatively stable patient/biological signal;
2. local healthcare-process signal.

Both may predict outcomes internally. Only the first is plausibly portable without additional assumptions. A deployment team needs a way to tell whether excellent validation performance is partly contingent on a source workflow that will not reproduce at the target site.

## What would make C008 nontrivial

A defensible paper must do more than:

- compare MIMIC with eICU;
- add missingness indicators/counts;
- report AUROC and calibration;
- show calibration gets worse;
- perform post-hoc recalibration.

Those elements are substantially occupied by R027 and R031.

### Candidate differentiators to prosecute

A serious C008 would need one or more of:

1. **Explicit shift attribution/decomposition** rather than generic source-target comparison.
2. **Controlled observation-process interventions/stress tests** that change recording policy while holding patient values/outcomes fixed as far as the design permits.
3. A **general evaluation framework across multiple tasks/environments/model classes**, not one sepsis model pair.
4. A defined **transportability-penalty quantity** tied to observation-process features.
5. Calibration and **clinical utility** as primary endpoints, with discrimination secondary.
6. Actionable diagnostic output: distinguish “recalibration may suffice” from “feature/workflow dependence makes transport unsafe.”

## Identification warning

“Effect of observation-process shift” can mean different things:

- a causal effect of changing measurement policy;
- a descriptive contrast under controlled masking/measurement perturbation;
- a decomposition of performance differences across observed environments;
- worst-case sensitivity over a prespecified family of observation shifts.

These are not interchangeable. C008 must select the estimand before analysis.

## Data feasibility

### Strongest initial substrate

- eICU-CRD for natural unit/hospital process heterogeneity [R043];
- MIMIC-IV as a well-understood external environment [R042];
- optional HiRID/AmsterdamUMCdb for a third environment [R045–R046].

### Feasibility advantage

The eICU demo permits early schema/code prototyping [R044] before full credentialed analysis.

## Current verdict

**SCREENING — priority lead.**

Confidence: **moderate**, not high.

Reason: the scientific problem is real and well aligned, but nearest-neighbor competition is unusually close and must be attacked before implementation.

# 12. Anti-gap conclusions

The following statements should **not** be made after this mapping pass:

- “Nobody has studied missingness shift.”
- “Nobody has studied clinical presence/observation-process shift.”
- “Clinical ML external validation ignores calibration.”
- “Nobody has examined MIMIC→eICU calibration drift.”
- “No one uses measurement counts/missingness as a source of domain shift.”
- “Post-deployment workflow telemetry is unexplored.”
- “Conformal prediction under distribution shift is an open methodological blank.”

These would be demonstrably weak novelty claims given [R024–R041].

# 13. What remains genuinely uncertain

1. Has a general **observation-process stress-test framework** already been published under different terminology?
2. Can source/target performance degradation be decomposed in a scientifically meaningful way without overclaiming causal attribution?
3. Does eICU provide sufficiently reliable site/unit metadata to separate interface/process variation from patient mix?
4. Are there multi-site tasks where observation-process variation is large enough to matter but outcome/feature definitions remain comparable?
5. Can controlled perturbations be made clinically realistic rather than arbitrary missingness masks?
6. Is there a transportability-penalty estimand that is interpretable enough to be useful to external validators?
7. Does R027 or its follow-up work already contain the strongest differentiators contemplated for C008?
8. What does the internal-external cross-validation / domain-generalization literature already contribute to OF-09?
9. How much of observation-process dependence is detectable without target labels?
10. When should an observation-process failure lead to feature removal/refit rather than recalibration?
